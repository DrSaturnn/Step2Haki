The Painful Hip in a Child: Septic Arthritis vs Transient Synovitis

Kocher criteria · how many are present, and has the hip been aspirated before antibiotics?

Red flagsScriptStem cuesDiagnosisManagementPreventionComplicationsPractice
Bottom line

Count the Kocher criteria. With 0 or 1, treat as transient synovitis: NSAIDs and a recheck in 24 to 48 hours. With 3 or more, obtain a blood culture and an ultrasound-guided hip aspiration before antibiotics. Septic fluid means surgical drainage and IV antibiotics the same day.

Exception: a child who is toxic or hypotensive gets antibiotics at once, with cultures drawn as they can be.

Chain: septic arthritis

Infection

→ bacteria reach the joint, usually through the blood

→ neutrophils release enzymes that digest cartilage

Structural

→ pus raises pressure inside the capsule

→ blood flow to the femoral head is cut off

Clinical

→ osteonecrosis and growth-plate damage within days

Distractors
Sequencing trapIV antibiotics before aspiration: septic arthritis is likely; antibiotics first can sterilize the culture. Aspirate first unless the child is unstable.
Mirror twinNSAIDs and discharge: the limp followed a cold; fever and refusal to bear weight are two Kocher points, not the benign twin.
Salient decoyMRI of the hip: it is the most detailed image; it delays drainage and belongs to suspected osteomyelitis.
Test-overrides-findingsNormal hip radiographs: they look reassuring; they are normal early and cannot exclude infection.
Redundant workupRadiographs after the ultrasound already showed a small effusion in a well child: another image answers no open question.
True-fact-wrong-slotAspirating a well child who walks: an effusion is present; with 0 or 1 criteria, the needle adds risk and no answer.
Prevention and screening
  • Routine Hib vaccination has made Haemophilus influenzae type b a rare cause.
  • Every child sent home as transient synovitis gets a set recheck and return precautions (above).
Complications and long-term sequelae
  • Septic arthritis: osteonecrosis of the femoral head, growth arrest with leg-length difference, hip dislocation, spread to bone, sepsis.
  • Transient synovitis: none expected; a limp that lasts is another disease until proven otherwise.
Practice
  1. 2-year-old girl with 1 day of irritability; cries when her diaper is changed and will not stand; T 39.2 C; holds the right leg flexed and turned outward; WBC 16,500/mm³; ESR 58 mm/h → Aspiration of the right hip → 4 of 4 Kocher criteria; the hip is never named
  2. 4-year-old boy limping since a cold last week, now T 39.0 C and refusing to stand; ESR 78 mm/h; WBC 14,500/mm³; hemodynamically stable → Blood culture and hip aspiration, then IV antibiotics → the cold does not make it benign; aspirate before antibiotics in a stable child
  3. 6-year-old boy with 2 days of limping and a cold 10 days ago; T 37.6 C; walks with a limp; pain only at the end of hip rotation; WBC 9,800/mm³; ESR 14 mm/h → Ibuprofen and recheck in 24 to 48 hours → 0 of 4 Kocher criteria
  4. 3-year-old boy, T 39.3 C, refuses to walk; lies with the right hip flexed, abducted and externally rotated and cries with any movement of it; WBC 17,000/mm³ → Septic arthritis of the hip → the position shows a hip effusion; fever and refusal to walk make it septic
  5. 5-year-old boy, well appearing and afebrile, walking with a limp for 2 days; ultrasound shows small effusions in both hips, although only the right hip hurts; WBC and ESR normal → Transient synovitis → effusions in both hips favor it; septic arthritis is one-sided
  6. 6-year-old girl, well appearing, afebrile and bearing weight; small effusions in both hips on ultrasound; WBC and CRP normal → Aspiration of both hips → 0 of 4 Kocher criteria; the needle adds risk and no answer
  7. 2-year-old girl with fever to 39.2 C who refuses to stand; ultrasound-guided hip aspiration returns cloudy fluid with 92,000 WBC/mm³, 90% neutrophils; Gram stain pending → Surgical drainage and IV antibiotics → septic arthritis of the hip; the hip cannot decompress itself
  8. 2-year-old boy with HbSS (sickle cell disease), T 39.4 C, refusing to bear weight on the left leg; ultrasound shows a left hip effusion → Salmonella → add a third-generation cephalosporin
  9. 7-year-old boy with 3 days of right knee pain and a limp; the knee has no swelling, tenderness or pain with movement; right hip internal rotation is limited and painful; afebrile → Ultrasound of both hips → hip pain referred to the knee
  10. 3-year-old boy, T 38.9 C, refusing to bear weight; WBC 14,000/mm³; ESR 52 mm/h; ultrasound shows a right hip effusion → Joint fluid cell count and culture → 3 of 4 Kocher criteria; the aspirate confirms
  11. 3-year-old girl, T 39.0 C, will not bear weight on the left leg; WBC 15,000/mm³; ESR 50 mm/h; hip radiographs are normal → Ultrasound-guided hip aspiration → radiographs are normal early and cannot exclude infection
  12. 5-year-old boy diagnosed 2 days ago with a post-viral limp and sent home on ibuprofen returns with T 39.1 C; he now refuses to stand; ESR 46 mm/h → Ultrasound-guided hip aspiration → the status changed: now 3 of 4 Kocher criteria
  13. 5-year-old boy treated with ibuprofen for a post-viral limp is still limping 4 weeks later; afebrile; labs normal → Hip radiographs → it should have resolved; look for Legg-Calvé-Perthes disease
  14. 6-year-old boy with a limp for 2 months; afebrile; hip internal rotation and abduction limited; labs normal → Legg-Calvé-Perthes disease → radiograph shows a flattened femoral head
  15. 13-year-old boy above the 95th percentile for weight with 3 weeks of thigh and knee pain; afebrile; the hip rotates outward when flexed, and internal rotation is lost → Slipped capital femoral epiphysis → frog-leg lateral radiographs of both hips
  16. 4-year-old boy, T 38.8 C, limping for 3 days; point tenderness over the lateral proximal thigh below the greater trochanter; passive hip rotation is full and only mildly painful → Proximal femoral osteomyelitis → MRI shows it
  17. 3-year-old boy whose septic arthritis of the hip was drained 4 days after symptoms began → Osteonecrosis of the femoral head → raised pressure in the capsule cuts off blood flow to the femoral head
  18. 6-year-old boy with a post-viral limp, afebrile and walking, 0 of 4 Kocher criteria, sent home on ibuprofen → Recheck in 24 to 48 hours → return sooner for fever or refusal to walk

Transferable rules

A limp after a cold is transient synovitis only if the child is afebrile and walking. Count the Kocher criteria first; with 3 or more, aspirate before any antibiotic in a stable child.

Before ordering another test, name the question it would answer and how the answer would change the next step.
