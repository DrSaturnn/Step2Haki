How to Use This

Method guide – the three habits, question verbs & the trap speciestap to expand

The three habits

  1. The assassin scan. For each answer choice, find the sentence in the stem that makes it impossible. When every option but one has an assassin, the question is finished – no forward reasoning required. A stem's "normal" findings are eliminations, not filler.
  2. The switch rule. Change an answer only when rereading reveals a misread fact – never when it reveals a new possibility. A detail that is merely compatible with another diagnosis is flavor. Incompatibility, not plausibility.
  3. Store the discriminator, not the outcome. Learn "limping child → score Kocher," never "limping child → aspirate." Rules stored as outcomes fire on the benign mirror-twin stem and get you twice.

Read the verb before the choices

  1. "Most likely diagnosis" → the diagnosis that explains all findings, not the most findings.
  2. "Most sensitive / screening" → lowest detection threshold. "Most accurate / definitive / confirms" → tissue, or the gold-standard test.
  3. "Next step in management" → the next rung on the ladder. "Next step in diagnosis" → the test whose result changes what you do tonight.
  4. "Most beneficial / reduces mortality" → match the intervention's time-to-benefit to the outcome's time horizon. Lipids = years. BP, volume, GDMT = weeks to months.
  5. "Underlying cause" → the trunk of the causal tree, not a branch.
The ten trap species
SpeciesHow it worksAntidote
Salient decoyA vivid, concrete detail – occupation, travel, family history – that is compatible with the right answer but suggests a wrong one. Camping in Georgia → Lyme, when the answer is rheumatic fever.Ask what job the detail is doing. If it's making you nervous rather than deciding anything, it's bait.
Treat-the-numberAn abnormal lab planted so you medicate the value instead of the outcome. Low HDL → niacin. High A1c → metformin. High Hct → phlebotomy.Ask which intervention changes the outcome named in the question.
Trump-card inversionA risk calculator offered when a categorical indication already decides it. 10-year risk 4.9% in a diabetic – diabetes already earned the statin.Check trump cards before calculators: prior event, LDL ≥190, diabetes 40–75.
True-fact-wrong-slotA real association placed where the timeline or findings forbid it. Post-URI in a 12-month history. Fibroids causing postmenopausal bleeding.Verify the interval and the physiologic state before you use the association.
Mirror twinThe benign version of a stem you just learned the dangerous version of. Transient synovitis right after septic arthritis.Store the scoring rule, not the action.
Sequencing trapA correct action offered one step out of order. MRI before conservative therapy. Antibiotics before aspiration. Imaging before airway.Name the ladder out loud before choosing a rung.
Uniform-answer doubtAn "all yes" or "no further testing" option rejected because it feels too easy. HIV vaccine grid – the answer really was yes to all four.Reject it only with a named disqualifying criterion you can say out loud.
Test-overrides-findingsA normal or discordant test offered against classic findings. Negative scaphoid film. Negative nitrite. Negative Phalen. Normal creatinine in early nephropathy. A nonfocal neuro exam in brain abscess.A negative result lowers probability according to the test's performance. It supports ruling out disease only when the remaining probability is sufficiently low for the decision.
Unchecked attributeThe right category is retrieved and then never checked against the one attribute that separates its members – direction, age, duration, grade, timing, or kind. The option is the correct disease family with the wrong value on the deciding axis.Name the attribute the stem specified, out loud, before choosing: which direction, at what age, over how long, at which grade.
Class-vs-memberThe right drug class represented by the wrong member, making a keyed answer look like it contradicts a guideline. "NSAIDs are first-line for OA" – but the only NSAID offered is ketorolac, a 5-day drug, so acetaminophen wins.Read which specific agent represents each class before rejecting the class.
The recurring failure mode Fast-and-wrong misses cluster where the deciding fact is in the background – the past medical history, the gestational age, the menopausal status, the duration – rather than in the foreground finding. Recognition speed is not the problem; the missing step is a deliberate 10-second completion pass: read the entire lab panel and the entire PMH before locking.

How NBME words questions

Patterns seen on the NBME form questions logged so far. Each line gives one paraphrased example and the brief that shows it.

Using the trap lines: each trap line in a brief names one wrong option, why it tempts, and the finding in the stem that rules it out.

  1. The diagnosis is given as its mechanism. A toddler with hip dysplasia found late is keyed as a poorly developed acetabulum, and a newborn with glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency as an enzyme deficiency. The Limping Child
  2. The organism is asked as a class. A painless genital ulcer is keyed as a spirochete, with obligate intracellular bacterium and gram-negative diplococcus beside it. Genital Ulcers
  3. A disease appears under a formal or older name. Kawasaki disease is listed as mucocutaneous lymph node syndrome, usually as a decoy. Acute Rheumatic Fever
  4. A decoy shares a name with a disease that would fit. Fanconi syndrome, a kidney tubule disorder, is offered among marrow failures; Fanconi anemia is the disease that would fit. Neutrophil Disorders: Number and Function
  5. The genetic defect and the parent of origin replace the syndrome. Angelman syndrome is keyed as a chromosome 15 deletion inherited from the mother, with the same deletion from the father as the next option. Imprinting Disorders: Angelman and Prader-Willi
  6. The key is a procedure or a way of measuring. Protein on a routine dipstick in a well adolescent is keyed as a repeat urinalysis at bedtime and on waking; a high automated blood pressure is keyed as a measurement by auscultation. Proteinuria and Nephrotic Syndrome in a Child
  7. An exposure is planted as bait. Cat scratches sit beside a cat-scratch disease option when the key is an infected thyroglossal duct cyst, and mosquito bites appear in a spotted fever stem. A refugee camp is different: it is a real risk factor for rheumatic fever, and it also invites travel-fever decoys. Lymphadenopathy
  8. Development comes as a grid. Each option is a row of delayed or normal for gross motor, fine motor and language, so every domain in the stem has to be read on its own. Developmental Milestones and Normal Growth
  9. The lead-in grants part of the answer. A bleeding newborn is asked what to give in addition to vitamin K, which leaves only the blood product to choose. Vitamins and Nutritional Deficiencies
  10. A definition may be dated. Older sources, including this NBME explanation, set adolescent hypertension at the 95th percentile at every age. The current 2017 American Academy of Pediatrics guideline uses a fixed cutoff of 130/80 mm Hg from age 13. High Blood Pressure in a Child or Adolescent
  11. Every wrong option is the treatment of a look-alike. A slipped capital femoral epiphysis is keyed as internal fixation, beside abduction bracing and spica casting (hip dysplasia), traction (fracture) and physical therapy (strain). Slipped Capital Femoral Epiphysis
  12. The finding is described, never named. A boy with a red, tender foot 3 weeks after a nail went through his shoe has osteomyelitis, but the word never appears; the question asks only for the organism. Foot Puncture Infection
  13. Each negative in the stem closes one decoy. In a newborn with two flank masses, an uncomplicated pregnancy, good Apgar scores and a normal hematocrit close renal vein thrombosis, which leaves polycystic kidney disease. Abdominal Mass in a Young Child
  14. One noun at different sites makes a location question. A virilized 10-year-old girl is offered adrenal, ovarian and pituitary adenoma. A high dehydroepiandrosterone sulfate (DHEAS) with a normal testosterone and a normal pelvic ultrasound places the source in the adrenal. Precocious Puberty
  15. A disease usually taught in its severe form appears mild. Posterior urethral valves appear in a well, feeding newborn with no Potter features, among four other causes of prenatal hydronephrosis. The thick bladder wall and a dribbling stream place the level. Posterior Urethral Valves
  16. The key is an umbrella term. A soft newborn murmur spreading to the lung fields and axilla is peripheral pulmonary stenosis, keyed as pulmonary stenosis because the specific name is not offered. Heart Murmurs in a Child
  17. The common answer is left off, and a planted sign fits a decoy. A 16-year-old smoker with past asthma admissions and severe hyperinflation is keyed as alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency because asthma is not an option. Clubbing and yellow sputum pull toward Kartagener syndrome. Obstructive Lung Disease
  18. A prevention lead-in skips the diagnosis. A recent immigrant with a rash that starts on the face is asked which vaccine would have prevented it. Measles-mumps-rubella (MMR) vaccine prevents both measles and rubella, so the two need not be separated. When the stem reads as rubella and the explanation teaches measles, the stem sets the facts. Fever and Rash in a Child
  19. The options are an age ladder, and the date is a cue. A 4-month-old seen in October is offered rotavirus, influenza, MMR, chickenpox and shingles. October fits influenza, but only rotavirus among the options is due at 4 months. Chickenpox and shingles are one virus listed twice. Childhood Vaccine Schedule and Contraindications
  20. The clue is in a relative. A boy with recurrent abscesses, osteomyelitis and pneumonia has a maternal uncle who died of fungal pneumonia, which points to X-linked chronic granulomatous disease. The key is written as impaired microbicidal activity. Neutrophil Disorders: Number and Function
  21. Nine options form a lab grid. An 18-month-old with poor growth, no vomiting or diarrhea, a normal blood pressure, low potassium, a normal-gap acidosis and an alkaline urine is offered nine causes of a potassium problem. Each option fills one cell of potassium (high or low), acid-base (acidosis or alkalosis) and blood pressure (high or normal); only renal tubular acidosis fills the low-potassium, acidosis, normal-pressure cell. Bartter syndrome appears under its tissue name, juxtaglomerular cell hyperplasia. Hypokalemia and Acid-Base Disorders in a Child

    Musculoskeletal & Rheumatology 17 topics + 3 board-style + 1 Aquifer

    The shelf's densest system. Nearly every stem is decided by a maneuver, a time course, or a red flag hiding in the past medical history.

    Pediatrics 3 topics + 2 board-style + 3 Aquifer

    Growth, development and the general-paediatric board questions that fit no single organ. Everything else paediatric is filed under its organ system and reachable from the Pediatrics shelf – age is a filter here, not a category.

    Cardiovascular 14 topics + 0 board-style + 0 Aquifer

    This system is where "treat the number" lives. Almost every miss here comes from medicating an abnormal lab instead of the outcome named in the question.

    Pulmonary 11 topics + 0 Aquifer

    Occupational and exposure histories dominate here – and they are as often bait as they are the answer. Check the lobe and the pleura before trusting the resume.

    Gastroenterology & Hepatology 13 topics + 4 board-style

    Pregnancy is a setting, not a diagnosis – and gestational age is the assassin that fires three times.

    Renal & Genitourinary 11 topics + 2 board-style + 1 Aquifer

    Two recurring lessons: normal values are eliminations, and a single discordant test component never overturns a convergent picture.

    Endocrine & Bone 9 topics + 0 board-style

    Two-step algorithms dominate: TSH then RAIU, and PTH then everything else. Know which variable is broken before you titrate to it.

    Hematology 6 topics + 1 board-style + 1 Aquifer

    Anemia is sorted by the smear and the reticulocyte count before any name is reached; the drug, the platelet count and the direct antiglobulin test decide the rest.

    Infectious Disease 11 topics + 6 board-style + 1 Aquifer

    Screening thresholds, drug toxicities, and sequencing. Isolation precedes medication; aspiration precedes antibiotics.

    Neurology & HEENT 11 topics + 3 board-style + 1 Aquifer

    Three questions triage every red eye. Two tuning forks triage every hearing loss.

    Dermatology & Wounds 5 topics + 1 board-style

    Morphology and depth decide these. Exposures and risk profiles suggest; the lesion in front of you decides.

    Obstetrics & Gynecology 8 topics + 0 board-style + 0 Aquifer

    Interval arithmetic and eligibility rules. The physiologic state of the patient decides which explanations are even permitted.

    Preventive Medicine & Ethics 4 topics + 0 board-style + 0 Aquifer

    Ranking questions and eligibility questions. Capacity determines the reporting path.

    Psychiatry & Behavioral Health 9 topics

    Mood, anxiety, psychotic, substance use and behavioral topics.

    Psych Pharmacology 18 of 18 class cards

    One short card per drug class, grouped as in the pharm documents. Each card holds what the drug does; the disease briefs keep the choice of drug.
