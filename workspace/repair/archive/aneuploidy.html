• Newborn with fetal growth restriction, microcephaly, a ventricular septal defect, hypertonia and clenched fists with the second digit overlapping the third → Trisomy 18 → Edwards syndrome — overlapping fingers are the hand sign
  • Which additional finding is expected in a newborn with growth restriction, microcephaly, VSD and overlapping fingers → Micrognathia → With prominent occiput, low-set pointed ears and rocker-bottom feet
  • Newborn with cutis aplasia of the scalp, microphthalmia, an omphalocele and holoprosencephaly → Trisomy 13 → Patau — the midline-defect trisomy
  • Infant with a high-pitched cat-like cry, microcephaly with a protruding metopic suture, hypertelorism and a flat nasal bridge → Cri-du-chat syndrome → 5p deletion — the cry is the name
  • Newborn girl with a webbed neck, low posterior hairline, broad chest with widely spaced nipples and cubitus valgus → Turner syndrome → 45,X — and coarctation, so check the femoral pulses
  • Hypotonic newborn with up-slanting palpebral fissures, epicanthal folds, Brushfield spots and a transverse palmar crease → Trisomy 21 → Down syndrome — hypotonia, not the hypertonia of trisomy 18
  • Why advanced maternal age raises the risk of trisomy 18 → Meiotic nondisjunction in oogenesis → Homologous chromosomes fail to separate, giving a disomic gamete
  • Newborn with dysmorphic features and suspected trisomy 18 → Full karyotype → FISH can suggest it but misses mosaicism
  • Trisomy 18 confirmed in a liveborn infant with a large VSD; the framework for care → Multidisciplinary supportive and palliative care → About 50% die within 2 weeks and 95% within the first year
  • Pregnant woman whose first-trimester screen shows low PAPP-A, low hCG and an enlarged nuchal translucency → Diagnostic karyotype by chorionic villus sampling → A positive screen always needs a diagnostic test
  • 18-hour-old boy born at home at about 40 weeks to a 19 yo mother who had no prenatal care; low tone since birth, a weak suck, and falls asleep after a few minutes at the breast; weight 2.3 kg; up-slanting palpebral fissures, open mouth with a protruding tongue, narrow intact palate; normal heart and lung examination, no hepatosplenomegaly; when held under the arms he nearly slips through the examiner's hands with his legs extended → Down syndrome → confirm with a karyotype showing an extra chromosome 21
  • 1-day-old boy born at 36 weeks after decreased fetal movement; mother declined prenatal testing; latches poorly; small face and jaw with a prominent occiput; pink oral mucosa with mildly bluish hands and feet; no palmar creases, overlapping fingers, convex soles and limited hip abduction; murmur at the left lower sternal border → Ventricular septal defect → Trisomy 18; holosystolic murmur that grows louder as pulmonary vascular resistance falls
  • 1-hour-old M, cyanosis at rest that clears with crying, no catheter passes either naris, coloboma, short lobeless ears, cleft palate, a harsh holosystolic murmur, undescended testes, normal head circumference → CHARGE syndrome → the eye and the nose name it
