  • A parent asks whether green discharge proves bacterial infection → Green discharge does not prove bacterial infection — colour reflects neutrophil myeloperoxidase in any inflammation
  • 7 yo M, 3 days of rhinorrhea, cough and mild maxillary pain, afebrile for 48 hours, well-appearing, profuse yellow-green nasal discharge with tender upper cheeks → Supportive care with saline irrigation and analgesics → viral rhinosinusitis
  • 3 yo boy with 2 weeks of nasal discharge, clear at first and now thick and foul-smelling; no fever, cough or breathing difficulty; eating normally; perinatally acquired HIV on antiretroviral therapy since birth, CD4 count 520 three months ago; vaccinations up to date; father smokes outdoors; a pet dog at home; growth at the 40th percentile; playful; purulent drainage from the right naris with red, swollen right mucosa and turbinates; left naris normal; normal oropharynx; no lymphadenopathy → Nasal foreign body → unilateral foul purulent discharge in a well, afebrile toddler; remove with positive pressure or instruments
