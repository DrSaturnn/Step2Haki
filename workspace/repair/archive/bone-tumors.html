• 13 year old with a firm painless mass above the knee for 3 weeks; x-ray shows a metaphyseal bony spur whose cortex is continuous with the adjacent bone. Without intervention → Benign growth until physeal closure → Osteochondroma — then static through adulthood, observation alone
  • Adolescent with a hard painless nodule near the knee; the spur points away from the joint and its medullary cavity is continuous with the bone → Osteochondroma → The most common benign bone tumour — cortical continuity is diagnostic
  • Adolescent boy with nocturnal thigh pain unrelated to activity and relieved by ibuprofen; film shows a small round lucency with sclerotic margins → Osteoid osteoma → Diaphyseal, with a central nidus
  • Untreated osteoid osteoma over the following years → Spontaneous involution → Which is why NSAIDs and serial films are reasonable
  • Why an osteoid osteoma hurts at night and answers to ibuprofen → Prostaglandin production by the nidus → NSAIDs inhibit prostaglandins, so the response is near-diagnostic
  • 15 year old with weeks of progressive knee pain and a palpable mass; metaphyseal film shows mixed lucency and density with a sunburst periosteal reaction → Osteosarcoma → The commonest primary bone malignancy of childhood, at the metaphysis
  • 12 year old with progressive night pain, fever and a mid-shaft femoral lesion; film shows a moth-eaten lytic lesion with onion-skin periosteal layers → Ewing sarcoma → Diaphyseal, and systemic features suggest metastatic disease
  • The genetic lesion underlying Ewing sarcoma → A t(11;22) EWSR1-FLI1 translocation → Producing a fusion transcription factor; small round blue cells
  • Child with a pathological fracture through a well-defined lucency with a thin sclerotic margin in the proximal humeral metaphysis, no periosteal reaction → Unicameral bone cyst → Medullary expansion thins the cortex; resolves at skeletal maturity
  • 28 year old with knee pain and an eccentric expansile lytic epiphyseal lesion with a soap-bubble appearance → Giant cell tumour of bone → Epiphyseal and after skeletal maturity; RANKL-driven
  • 16 year old with constant knee pain and a well-circumscribed epiphyseal lucency with a thin sclerotic rim that crosses the physis → Chondroblastoma → Epiphyseal but skeletally immature — the age separates it from giant cell tumour
  • 14 yo F, 5 weeks of steadily worsening left hip pain, now with difficulty climbing stairs, plus intermittent fever and fatigue; no trauma or chronic illness; T 38.3 C; warm, tender swelling over the left iliac crest; x-ray shows a lucent lesion of the left ilium with layered periosteal reaction; biopsy shows sheets of small, uniform round cells with scant cytoplasm → Ewing sarcoma → pelvis and diaphysis, with fever and high inflammatory markers that mimic osteomyelitis; MRI for extent, biopsy confirms; chemotherapy plus resection and/or radiation
