## Q22 Beckwith-Wiedemann: hepatoblastoma (peds-cms-f627e3)

- Sort: **new angle**
- Owning brief: `bs-abdominal-mass` (Abdominal Mass in a Young Child, data-bp `renal multi`). Pairs with `tumor-syndromes`, which owns Beckwith-Wiedemann recognition (q_086641adf419daa39e0b, key Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome; no tumor choice).
- Why not covered: the brief lists hepatoblastoma in its differential table (liver origin, Beckwith-Wiedemann association, raised AFP) and in its workup table (serum AFP by branch), but no scenario item has hepatoblastoma as the key. All six items key Wilms, neuroblastoma, MCDK or pheochromocytoma. The new look-alike is Wilms tumor inside the syndrome that carries both tumors.
- What must be taught:
  - Deciding finding: a firm right upper quadrant mass with a very high serum AFP in an infant with Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome points to hepatoblastoma. The syndrome raises the risk of both Wilms tumor and hepatoblastoma, so the syndrome alone cannot pick between them; the AFP and the liver location do.
  - Key: hepatoblastoma.
  - Trapline, Wilms tumor (nephroblastoma): the famous Beckwith-Wiedemann tumor and the salient decoy. It is a flank mass in the kidney and does not raise AFP.
  - Trapline, neuroblastoma: at 6 months the age fits it better than Wilms (the page's own rule is "usually before age 2"), but urine HVA and VMA are normal. Trapline, germ cell (yolk sac) tumor: it shares the raised AFP but presents as a pelvic mass, not a firm upper abdominal one. Spina bifida occulta is AFP bait: raised AFP goes with open neural tube defects, not the occult form.
- NBME framing lesson: the lead-in asks for the "explanation for the mass" with the syndrome already named, and the tumor markers come as a lab panel you have to read against the reference ranges (AFP very high, the catecholamine metabolites normal). Wilms tumor is listed under its formal name, nephroblastoma.
- Conflict with page facts: none. The page's AFP row (raised with a liver mass, hepatoblastoma) and its Wilms row (Beckwith-Wiedemann association) agree with the NBME explanation.

## Q23 Acute rheumatic fever (peds-cms-e6a460)

- Sort: **covered**
- Owning brief: `arf` (Acute Rheumatic Fever, data-bp `cv immune`).
- Item that carries the decision: **q_163add7fdad553c2a5e2** (joint arthritis plus a new apical holosystolic murmur leads to acute rheumatic fever). The "setting is not a mechanism" variant is q_a0a18460905c5eb98320 (a camping-trip decoy).
- Same decision: the key is the same, and so is the deciding finding: large-joint polyarthritis with fever plus an apical holosystolic murmur radiating to the axilla (mitral regurgitation, meaning carditis). That is 2 major Jones criteria.
- "How NBME framed it" line to add: the stem never mentions a sore throat. The only clue to strep is a refugee-camp history, and that same line also recruits the travel-fever decoys (dengue, malaria, typhoid, plus Lyme). Kawasaki appears under its formal name, mucocutaneous lymph node syndrome. The murmur is described by site and radiation, not named as mitral regurgitation.
- Conflict with page facts: none. The NBME explanation matches the page on type II hypersensitivity through M-protein mimicry, mitral involvement, and salicylates plus long-term monthly penicillin. NBME lists leukocytosis among the minor criteria, while the page lists ESR/CRP. That affects no key, so no fix is needed.

## Q24 Primary syphilis: organism class (peds-cms-42419d)

- Sort: **new**
- Proposed brief: `bs-genital-ulcer` (Genital Ulcer in an Adolescent), system reproductive/infectious, data-bp `repro_f`. It pairs with `bs-fever-rash-arthralgia`, which owns secondary syphilis (a palms-and-soles rash) and mentions the painless chancre only as history, and with `cervicitis` and `bs-pid` for the chlamydia and gonorrhea decoys.
- Why no owner: a page grep finds no chancroid, no H. ducreyi, no "spirochete" and no genital ulcer differential. The only "chancre" on the page is a timing note in the secondary syphilis row of bs-fever-rash-arthralgia. No item keys primary syphilis or an ulcer's organism.
- What must be taught:
  - Deciding finding: a single painless (nontender) genital ulcer with raised borders in a sexually active adolescent, with no systemic signs, is a primary syphilis chancre. Painless is the discriminator.
  - Key: spirochete (Treponema pallidum). The NBME explanation adds that the RPR can be falsely negative at this stage, that dark-field microscopy of chancre fluid is the most sensitive test, and that treatment is one IM dose of benzathine penicillin G with testing for other STIs.
  - Trapline, gram-negative bacillus (H. ducreyi, chancroid): the look-alike ulcer, but it is painful and comes with inguinal lymphadenopathy. Trapline, virus (HSV): the lesions are painful, start as grouped vesicles and then crust.
  - Trapline, obligate intracellular bacterium (C. trachomatis) and gram-negative diplococcus (N. gonorrhoeae): these are the STIs most taught on the page, but they cause cervicitis, urethritis and PID, not a painless ulcer. Protozoan (Trichomonas) causes a frothy malodorous discharge, not an ulcer.
- NBME framing lesson: the options are organism classes ("spirochete", "gram-negative bacillus", "obligate intracellular bacterium"), and the disease is never named. You have to name the ulcer, then translate the organism into its class, and each class hides a different STI.
- Conflict with page facts: none. Note: intake_table.md labels this item "Secondary syphilis". The source (a painless chancre, no rash) and the NBME explanation make it **primary** syphilis, so the table heading should read primary.

## Q25 Orthostatic proteinuria (peds-cms-6b35bd)

- Sort: **new**
- Proposed brief: `bs-isolated-proteinuria` (Incidental Proteinuria in a Well Child), system renal, data-bp `renal`. It pairs with `nephrotic-child` (which owns proteinuria with edema, steroids and biopsy) and with `hematuria` (whose q_8168984f488156458e4c is the parallel "well child with an isolated urine finding" item).
- Why no owner: the page has no mention of orthostatic, postural or transient proteinuria, and no split, first-morning or recumbent urine collection. Every proteinuria item on the page is nephrotic or nephritic, with edema, hypertension, hematuria or an abnormal creatinine. A fallback host would be a look-alike row in nephrotic-child, but that brief's decision is a different one.
- What must be taught:
  - Deciding finding: 3+ protein found incidentally on a routine urinalysis in a well, asymptomatic adolescent boy with a normal exam and normal blood pressure. No edema, hematuria or illness means it is probably benign (transient or orthostatic) until it proves persistent.
  - Key: repeat the urinalysis, once at bedtime and once on waking. Orthostatic proteinuria shows protein in the upright (bedtime) sample and a normal first-morning (recumbent) sample. It is common in adolescent boys. Only persistent proteinuria earns a renal workup.
  - Trapline, trial of corticosteroids and renal biopsy: these belong to nephrotic or nephritic syndrome (the nephrotic-child ladder). Here the diagnosis is not yet established, and biopsy is invasive and carries real risk.
  - Trapline, renal ultrasonography and IV pyelography: these are anatomic studies for obstruction, masses, cysts or strictures. Neither answers whether the proteinuria is real and persistent.
- NBME framing lesson: the key is written as a procedure ("repeat urinalysis at bedtime and on awakening") that never names orthostatic proteinuria. It sits against escalating workup options, and the stem hides the answer by making the child entirely well.
- Conflict with page facts: none (the page is silent).

## Q26 Developmental dysplasia of the hip: mechanism (peds-cms-e52ce6)

- Sort: **new angle**
- Owning brief: `limp` (The Limping Child — Master Table, data-bp `msk`). It has a DDH row, and item q_bdee86ce98775e1e9a3f keys DDH in a 12-month-old with breech delivery, asymmetric thigh folds and a positive Barlow. `bs-torticollis` owns the breech-crowding association (q_fa278e2e3e52501580b1, q_d667351ae9a15bde8a37).
- Why not covered: the existing item is a diagnosis question decided by Barlow and thigh folds. This NBME item asks for a **mechanism**, and its look-alike is the late-presenting walker: a painless limp since walking began, a shorter leg and limited hip abduction, with no Barlow or Ortolani. A page grep finds no leg-length discrepancy, Galeazzi sign or "poorly developed acetabulum", and the only "acetabul" hit is in `hippos` (adult fracture/dislocation).
- What must be taught:
  - Deciding finding: a girl born breech, now a toddler with a painless limp since she began walking, a shortened leg and limited abduction of that hip. That is untreated DDH presenting late.
  - Key: a poorly developed (dysplastic) acetabulum, which is the mechanism behind femoral head instability. Per the NBME explanation, the risk factors are female sex, breech and family history. Barlow/Ortolani and ultrasound are for young infants (ultrasound under about 4 months), and the Pavlik harness is the infant treatment. Untreated DDH leads to disability, pain and early osteoarthritis.
  - Trapline, displacement of the capital femoral epiphysis: this is SCFE written as a mechanism, an adolescent disease with loss of internal rotation. Trapline, failure of osteoid to mineralize: this is rickets written as a mechanism, which gives bowed legs from vitamin D deficiency. Both are name-swap decoys, the diseases stated only by their mechanisms.
  - Trapline, acute epiphyseal fracture, acute suppurative arthritis and avulsion of muscle origin: each is acute and painful (trauma, or fever with a septic joint), which a painless limp present for 4 months rules out.
- NBME framing lesson: every option is written as a mechanism rather than a disease name, so you must name the disease (DDH) first and then translate it. The stem also removes the infant exam maneuvers and gives the walker's signs (shortened leg, limited abduction) instead.
- Conflict with page facts: none on the key. There is a framing gap: the page's limp decision spine says "infant DDH; 1–3 toddler's fracture", which would steer a 16-month-old away from DDH. The backfill should add a line saying that DDH missed in infancy presents in a walker as a painless limp with a shortened leg and limited abduction. Source quirk (not a page issue): the stem calls it a right-sided limp but puts the short leg and limited abduction on the left. Record it; do not copy it.

## Q27 Chlamydia/gonorrhea screening in a sexually active adolescent (peds-cms-5c434b)

- Sort: **new angle**
- Owning brief: `cervicitis` (Acute Cervicitis, data-bp `repro_f`). It owns C. trachomatis/N. gonorrhoeae and NAAT for both organisms, and it lists "age under 25" and "asymptomatic most commonly" among its risk factors and manifestations. The Pap smear decoy is owned by `bs-cervical-gate` (q_4e03aecd840d573fbd70: under 21 means no cervical screening, whatever the sexual history).
- Why not covered: cervicitis items are symptomatic diagnosis and treatment (empiric ceftriaxone plus doxycycline, azithromycin in pregnancy, PID ascent). No item on the page keys routine screening of an asymptomatic sexually active young woman. The page's NAAT items are diagnostic (hematuria q_6b927bfb883a56499156, sterile pyuria), not screening. The new task is screening.
- What must be taught:
  - Deciding finding: an asymptomatic, sexually active girl under 25 at a health maintenance visit, with a normal exam and no discharge. USPSTF screens every sexually active woman under 25 for both organisms, because the infection is often silent and screening prevents sequelae (PID). The two organisms often coinfect.
  - Key: NAAT for C. trachomatis and N. gonorrhoeae.
  - Trapline, Pap smear: cervical cytology starts at 21 whatever the sexual history (the bs-cervical-gate rule). Trapline, mammography: the mother's breast cancer at 40 is a planted decoy, and the NBME explanation places mammography screening after age 40.
  - Trapline, fasting glucose and hemoglobin: a BMI of 25 alone does not meet the adolescent diabetes-screening bar (overweight plus added risk factors such as insulin resistance), and USPSTF does not recommend routine iron-deficiency anemia screening.
- NBME framing lesson: the stem plants one cue per decoy (the mother's breast cancer for mammography, the BMI for glucose, the irregular menses for hemoglobin, the sexual activity for Pap), and the key is written out in full as a test (nucleic acid amplification testing, naming both organisms by genus and species) rather than as "STI screening".
- Conflict with page facts: none. The Pap interval in the NBME explanation (cytology every 3 years from 21; co-testing every 5 years from 30) matches bs-cervical-gate.
