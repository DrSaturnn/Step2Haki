<h4>Infant Stool Complaints: Dyschezia, FPIAP &amp; Secondary Lactase Deficiency</h4>
<p class="sub">The well-appearing child decides the tier · reassure, eliminate, or work up</p>
<div class="vignette"><span class="lbl">Source vignette</span> <b>Pt</b> &mdash; 4 mo M, thriving, screams and strains for 10 minutes several times daily then passes a soft yellow stool, normal examination &middot; <b>Q</b> &mdash; the most appropriate next step; options spanned reassurance and parental education, a glycerin suppository and switching to an extensively hydrolyzed formula</div>
<div class="dp"><span class="lbl">The decision point</span>
<p>Three benign stool complaints share one exam shape — a <b>thriving, well-appearing</b> infant or child whose parents are alarmed — and the answer is set by the <b>stool</b> and the <b>timing</b>, not by the alarm. Straining and screaming for minutes, then a <b>normal soft stool</b>, under 9 months, is <b>infant dyschezia</b> → reassurance only. <b>Painless blood-streaked stools</b> in a well infant under 6 months is <b>food protein-induced allergic proctocolitis</b> → eliminate cow's milk and soy from the maternal diet, or an extensively hydrolyzed formula. Watery diarrhea and bloating <b>after dairy is reintroduced</b> following gastroenteritis is <b>secondary lactase deficiency</b> → temporary lactose avoidance, no workup.</p>
<p>The two mimics that change the tier are both <b>ill-appearing</b>: <b>FPIES</b> (profuse vomiting and lethargy 1–4 h after the trigger, non-IgE) needs fluids, and <b>IgE-mediated</b> allergy (urticaria, wheeze, anaphylaxis within minutes) needs epinephrine. The miss is treating the parents' distress as a finding: a formula change, a suppository, or a stool workup for a thriving infant is the wrong rung.</p></div>

<p class="sub">A teen with hepatitis, a tremor and brown corneal rings &middot; is the patient young, with brain, eye or red-cell findings, and is the ceruloplasmin low?</p>

<div class="danger"><span class="lbl">Management</span> <b>Symptomatic disease</b> &mdash; copper chelation with <b>penicillamine or trientine</b>, lifelong, with a low-copper diet (avoid shellfish, organ meats, nuts, chocolate and mushrooms) &middot; <b>Maintenance or presymptomatic disease</b> &mdash; <b>zinc</b>, which blocks intestinal copper absorption &middot; <b>Fulminant liver failure</b> &mdash; urgent <b>liver transplantation</b>, which corrects the liver defect &middot; <b>Family</b> &mdash; screen first-degree relatives, <b>siblings</b> above all (a 1 in 4 chance of disease, since it is autosomal recessive), with ceruloplasmin, liver tests and a slit-lamp exam &middot; <b>Never stop</b> &mdash; stopping therapy can precipitate acute liver failure</div>
<div class="pearls"><span class="lbl">Pearls</span> Psychiatric change or falling school performance can precede the liver findings, so a young patient with new psychiatric illness and abnormal liver tests needs a ceruloplasmin &middot; Acute liver failure with a Coombs-negative hemolytic anemia in a teen or young adult is fulminant Wilson disease, and it needs a transplant center, not chelation alone &middot; A presumed autoimmune hepatitis in a young patient should have ceruloplasmin checked before steroids &middot; Serum total copper is usually low; the urine copper is the number that is high</div>
<div class="pearls"><span class="lbl">Pairs with</span> Part I <b>MASLD / Metabolic Fatty Liver</b> lists Wilson disease among the exclusions: is the patient under about 40, with tremor or Kayser-Fleischer rings, and is the ceruloplasmin low? Part I <b>Cholestasis &amp; the LFT Patterns</b> places Wilson in the hepatocellular pattern: are the transaminases, not the alkaline phosphatase, leading? Part II <b>Hereditary Spherocytosis</b> owns the other Coombs-negative hemolysis of the young: are there spherocytes and an affected parent, or liver disease with corneal rings? Part I <b>Tick-Borne Fever and Rash</b> holds the acute mimic: did confusion with transaminitis arrive over hours with fever and a rash, or over months with a tremor?</div>
<div class="rule"><span class="lbl">Transferable rule</span> When liver disease in a patient under about 40 comes with a finding outside the liver, ask which organ it is: brain, cornea or red cells point to copper, the lung to alpha-1 antitrypsin, and the pancreas, skin, joints and heart in an older patient to iron.</div>
